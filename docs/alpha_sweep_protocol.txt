REAL-TEXT SMOOTHING SENSITIVITY, VERSION 1

Purpose
Test how smoothing changes the size response, how well both analytic laws
track it, and whether the change appears in rare-row and coverage diagnostics.
This is a pre-specified sensitivity study on existing source allocations.
The alpha=5 outcomes are known. No independent fresh-corpus confirmation or
external preregistration is claimed. All planned conditions will be reported.
No neural training is performed. Results belong in a new, separate appendix.

Design
Reuse all three corpora, 20 A/B pairs and six reference banks from
rare_context_prospective_v1. Sizes are nested 512/1024/2048/4096 chunks per arm.
Reference sizes are nested 1024/2048/4096/8192 chunks per bank. Reuse the same
128 evaluation chunks, q=0.5 masks, unigram, auxiliary source partition,
radius two and trace-scaled ridge coefficient .01.
Alpha grid: .5,1,2,5,10,20,50. Primary arm fixes all mask-pattern weights at
their original alpha=5 values. Robustness arm recomputes auxiliary Gram and
cross-products at each alpha using the original auxiliary mask and ridge rule.
Weights in that arm are shared by A and B and all sizes/references at that alpha.
Report evaluation-pattern-weighted squared weight norms.

Methods
Uncorrected full source and smoothed multinomial, plus source-split jackknife
of both, over the full grid. Retain same-lag source estimates as diagnostics.
Nonlinear source bootstrap and its jackknife at alpha=1,5,20, both weight arms,
and every target/reference size. Use three batches of sixteen draws. Sources
are resampled jointly across lags until n chunks, truncating only the last
source. Preserve the original source partitions and seed rule from the
correction study and follow-up. Common draws across alpha/weight arms reduce
Monte Carlo noise but do not eliminate it. Report batch Monte Carlo SEs.
Jackknife source fractions count sources, not chunks. Store raw estimates,
including negative values. Never silently clip before taking a logarithm.
Nonpositive scalar forecasts are reported as failures with undefined log error.

Sequence
1. Save this protocol/configuration before new-alpha A/B scoring.
2. Check source identities, original alpha=5 outputs, dense/sparse identities,
   whole-source sampling and the fixed-weight large-alpha limit. Timing pilots
   use reference data or synthetic inputs, not new-alpha A/B outcomes.
3. Save code and input hashes, reference-only diagnostics, and ALL forecasts
   for ALL corpora/arms/alpha values before new-alpha A/B scoring.
4. Score every planned pair, compute summaries using the specified resampling,
   then write a separate appendix. Subsequent bug fixes must retain a dated
   record and state whether outcomes had already been scored.

Primary comparisons
Within each corpus, beta is OLS slope of log(arithmetic mean pair disagreement)
on log n over the four sizes. Forecast beta uses log(arithmetic mean bank
forecast), primarily m=8192 uncorrected and secondarily m=2048 jackknife.
H1: beta(20)-beta(1)>0 for the fixed-weight arm. Report paired 95% percentile
intervals and effect magnitudes for each corpus. Consistent positive intervals
across all three corpora support the directional prediction. Other alpha
contrasts and the refit arm are secondary. An imprecise interval is inconclusive.
H2: For each law and corpus, average absolute forecast-slope error over seven
alphas is smaller than the error of beta=-1. Report their paired difference
and 95% interval. Report every alpha cell and its 512-to-2048 percentage decline.
An interval containing regression slope one is not an accuracy criterion.
Identity scatterplots are descriptive. Neither 21 cells nor evaluation chunks
are treated as independent experimental replications.

Reference and row diagnostics
Before scoring, report query-weighted reference counts scaled by n/m: fraction
below alpha and mean alpha/(scaled_count+alpha), for each bank/alpha/size.
Bin queried lag/context rows by their per-chunk rate in auxiliary TRAINING
counts, independently of A/B and reference counts. Auxiliary-unseen rows have
their own bin. Fixed bin edges are in the configuration. A bin's rate location
is the query-weighted geometric mean of its positive auxiliary rates.
For each pair/alpha/size/weight arm, compute exact weighted same-lag energies
per bin, signed cross-lag energy, and same-lag energies for contexts seen in
both A/B or in exactly one. Both-unseen contributions are zero. Check their
additive identities against full observed disagreement. Coverage shares use
same-lag energy as denominator. They are not shares of full energy with
cross-lag interactions allocated implicitly.
Adjacent-size bin elasticities use the same bin members and geometric-mean n.
Zero-energy bins have undefined elasticity, not artificial epsilon values.
Compare with the Poisson conditional-multinomial total-variance identity.
For its illustrative curves use auxiliary empirical conditional probabilities
on observed rows and calculate row-level curves before query-weighted bin
aggregation. Auxiliary-unseen rows have no identified conditional curve.
Signs near lambda=alpha and collapse versus lambda/alpha are diagnostics,
not necessary properties of dependent real text. The random-row-total term
includes fluctuations among positive counts as well as zero/nonzero coverage.

Quality and inference
Report full-vocabulary MSE, relative MSE improvement and exact top-1 accuracy
for both arms and every alpha/size. Break score ties by the smallest token ID.
Average masked positions within chunks, chunks within model, then A/B models
and pairs equally. Disagreement averages chunks and then pairs equally.
For 5000 draws, resample six banks and twenty pairs independently within each
corpus, using the same indices for all alphas, sizes and methods. Recompute
means and slopes inside each draw. Observed-only slope intervals resample pairs.
Auxiliary/evaluation inputs and saved bootstrap draws remain fixed. Pair nesting
and bank nesting are preserved. Report calibration ratios and mean bank absolute
log error. Small-reference primary calibration cells remain m<n from the
original study, with full-grid and m>n results also reported.

Interpretation
Increasing alpha need not monotonically reduce realized or expected A/B
disagreement when row counts fluctuate. Only fixed-weight convergence to the
shared unigram at sufficiently large alpha is a general sanity check here.
No monotonicity requirement is used to filter outcomes. A negative or weak H1
result narrows the smoothing explanation. Different behavior after refitting
weights distinguishes direct smoothing from auxiliary adaptation. Calibration
rankings and coverage trends are exploratory, not predetermined winners.
